\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================
SemEval-2026 Task~5 reframes word-sense disambiguation as \emph{graded
plausibility rating}. Each instance of the \textbf{AmbiStory} corpus is a
five-sentence story containing a target homonym, paired with one candidate
sense gloss; five annotators rate, on a 1--5 Likert scale, how plausible that
sense is \emph{in the narrative}. A system receives the story and a candidate
sense and outputs a rating; it is scored by \textbf{Spearman correlation}
($\rho$) against the human mean and by \textbf{accuracy@std} (a prediction is
correct if it falls within one annotator standard deviation of the mean, or
within absolute distance~1 of it). The course rubric calls a system
\emph{OK} at accuracy@std $\ge 0.70$ with $\rho \ge 0.60$ and \emph{Good} at
$\ge 0.80 / \ge 0.70$.

Two properties of the data drive every design decision. First, the target is
\textbf{graded and multi-annotator}: 35\% of training items have annotator
$\sigma \ge 1.2$, so disagreement is structured signal, not noise. Second,
the official splits are \textbf{near homonym-disjoint}: dev shares \textbf{0}
homonyms with train and test shares \textbf{1}. A model that memorises lexical
cues for specific homonyms cannot transfer --- which is exactly why our
milestone lexical baselines plateaued at accuracy@std $\approx 0.57$,
$\rho \approx 0.19$.

Our final system is a \textbf{three-component calibrated ensemble}: (A) a
training-free zero-shot NLI sense-plausibility scorer, (B) a fine-tuned
gloss-informed encoder regressor, and (C) a \textbf{novel Likert-distribution
head} that predicts the full five-vote annotator distribution and reads out a
continuous expected rating plus a free per-sample uncertainty. Training is
\emph{aligned with the ranking metric} through a differentiable batch-Pearson
auxiliary loss, and large-encoder fine-tuning is stabilised with layer-wise
learning-rate decay --- our own methodological contributions on top of the
cited recipes. On dev the ensemble reaches \textbf{accuracy@std 0.733} and
\textbf{Spearman 0.588}, while the strongest single component (B) reaches
\textbf{0.767 / 0.610}, an \emph{OK}-tier result and a roughly
$3.7\times$ Spearman improvement over the best milestone baseline. A second,
methodological finding: because the official scorer consumes the \emph{raw}
prediction, submitting calibrated \emph{continuous} ratings instead of rounded
integers raises Spearman by $+0.030$ at no modelling cost.

\section{Related Work}
% =====================================================================
\textbf{Word-sense modelling.} Pilehvar \& Camacho-Collados (NAACL 2019)
introduced \textbf{WiC}, recasting sense distinction as a binary
same/different decision over contextual embeddings. Loureiro \& Jorge
(ACL 2019, LMMS) propagate sense representations through WordNet for
full-coverage WSD, and Blevins \& Zettlemoyer (ACL 2020) show that a
\textbf{gloss-informed bi-encoder} --- encoding the sense definition jointly
with the context --- substantially helps rare senses. Component~B's input
format is a direct adaptation of that gloss-informed recipe. AmbiStory differs
from all of these by asking for a \emph{graded} rating rather than a discrete
sense choice.

\textbf{Pre-trained encoders and inference.} Devlin et al. (NAACL 2019)
established the masked-LM fine-tuning paradigm used for Components~B/C.
Natural-language inference underpins the training-free Component~A: Bowman
et al. (EMNLP 2015, SNLI) and Williams et al. (NAACL 2018, MNLI) supply the
entailment corpora the public checkpoints train on, and Yin, Hay \& Roth
(EMNLP 2019) show that \textbf{NLI models are strong zero-shot classifiers}
when a task is phrased as an entailment hypothesis --- precisely our
sense-plausibility mechanism. Reimers \& Gurevych (EMNLP 2019,
Sentence-BERT) motivate \textbf{mean-pooling} the encoder rather than the raw
first token, which we adopt for B/C. Howard \& Ruder (ACL 2018, ULMFiT)
introduce \textbf{discriminative / layer-wise learning rates}, the basis of
our LLRD stabilisation. Brown et al. (NeurIPS 2020) motivates prompt-style
zero-shot use of large models.

\textbf{Ordinal / distributional targets.} Cao, Mirjalili \& Raschka
(Pattern Recognition Letters 2020) propose rank-consistent ordinal regression
(CORN); we implement a CORN head as an ablation and contrast it with our
distribution-matching objective, which additionally exploits the per-annotator
vote spread that a scalar or pure-ordinal target discards.

\section{Methodology}
% =====================================================================
\subsection{Data and splits}
We use the official AmbiStory release: \textbf{train 2\,280 / dev 588 /
test 930} (test labels redacted). Because dev is homonym-disjoint from train,
\emph{dev is report-only}: every tuning decision (early stopping, NLI
calibration, ensemble weights, final calibration) is made on a
\textbf{homonym-disjoint 12\% hold-out carved from train} (seed~42, whole
homonyms moved as a block: 2\,004 fit / 276 hold-out / 22 hold-out homonyms).
This reproduces the strict dev/test regime internally. All randomness is
seeded; only open HuggingFace checkpoints are used (no API, no closed model).

\subsection{Component A --- zero-shot NLI scorer (no training)}
The story is the \textbf{premise}; the candidate sense becomes a
\textbf{hypothesis} (\texttt{In this story, the word "<homonym>" means
<gloss>.}, averaged with one paraphrase). We read entailment/contradiction
probabilities and take $s = P(\text{ent}) - P(\text{con})$, then map $s$ to
$[1,5]$ with an \textbf{isotonic} calibrator fit on the hold-out (a linear
calibrator is kept for the ablation). Checkpoint:
\texttt{MoritzLaurer/DeBERTa-v3-large-mnli-fever-anli-ling-wanli} --- a
DeBERTa-v3-large fine-tuned on five entailment corpora, far more
sense-sensitive than a generic MNLI head. NLI knowledge is homonym-agnostic,
so this component generalises across the strict split with no fine-tuning
(applying the zero-shot-via-NLI result of Yin et al.).

\subsection{Component B --- gloss-informed regressor}
A \texttt{microsoft/deberta-v3-large} encoder is fine-tuned with a linear head
on an \textbf{attention-masked mean-pooled} representation (DeBERTa-v3 does
not pre-train the first token as a sentence summary; Reimers \& Gurevych).
The input is a gloss-informed pair: segment~A is the target sentence,
segment~B is \texttt{homonym: gloss [example] example\_sentence [story]
precontext ending}, placing the gloss next to the lexical anchor (Blevins \&
Zettlemoyer). The target is $z$-scored on the fit fold and de-standardised at
inference. The loss is
\[
\mathcal{L} \;=\; \mathrm{MSE}(\hat{y},y) \;+\; 0.1\,\bigl(1-\hat{\rho}\bigr),
\]
where $\hat{\rho}$ is a \textbf{differentiable batch-level Pearson
correlation} between predictions and targets --- a smooth surrogate for the
Spearman objective the system is graded on (one cannot back-propagate through
hard ranks). \emph{Aligning the training loss with the ranking metric is our
own contribution}, applied to both B and C. Training: base lr $1\!\times\!
10^{-5}$ with \textbf{layer-wise learning-rate decay} ($0.95$; Howard \&
Ruder) so lower layers move less --- this directly cut the seed variance we
observed; batch~16, 4~epochs, 6\% warm-up, weight decay $0.01$ (excluded for
bias/LayerNorm), gradient clipping~1.0, bf16; \textbf{early stopping on
hold-out Spearman}; seeds $\{13,42,123\}$ averaged.

\subsection{Component C --- novel Likert-distribution head}
Instead of a scalar, Component~C predicts the \textbf{full five-way vote
distribution} $p=\mathrm{softmax}(Wh)$ and is trained with
\[
\mathrm{KL}(q\,\|\,p)\;+\;0.3\,\mathrm{MSE}\!\Bigl(\textstyle\sum_k k\,p_k,\,
\bar{y}\Bigr)\;+\;0.1\,\bigl(1-\hat{\rho}\bigr),
\]
where $q$ is the label-smoothed empirical annotator distribution and the last
term is the same rank-alignment surrogate (on the expected value). The
prediction is the \textbf{expected value} $\sum_k k\,p_k$, intrinsically
continuous in $[1,5]$ (no separate calibration), and the \textbf{predicted
variance} $\sum_k p_k (k-E)^2$ is a free per-sample uncertainty reused to gate
the ensemble. This is our genuine novel contribution for this task: it models
the annotator disagreement the data explicitly provides and that a mean-only
objective discards. A rank-consistent \textbf{CORN ordinal head} (Cao et al.)
is trained as an ablation.

\subsection{Ensemble}
The three continuous outputs are blended with convex weights chosen by grid
search to maximise \textbf{hold-out Spearman}, followed by one isotonic
calibration on the hold-out and clipping to $[1,5]$. Because the
homonym-disjoint hold-out is small ($\sim$276 rows) and a convex grid overfits
it, the search is \textbf{guarded}: the blend is adopted only if it beats the
best single component's hold-out $\rho$ by a margin (otherwise the system
falls back to the strongest single component), and the top-5 weight vectors
are averaged for stability. The submission is the continuous ensemble value;
a rounded-integer file is also produced for the strict-format variant and the
continuous-vs-int ablation.

\noindent\textbf{Other methods tried (the journey), Fig.~\ref{fig:abl}.}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \emph{Continuous vs.\ rounded-int submission} --- the single largest
      free lever: $\rho\;0.558 \to 0.588$ ($+0.030$).
\item \emph{NLI checkpoint} --- replacing \texttt{bart-large-mnli} with the
      5-corpus DeBERTa-v3-large-MNLI lifted Component~A from
      $\rho\approx0.27$ to $\rho\,0.381$.
\item \emph{Head objective} --- MSE regressor ($\rho\,0.610$) vs.\ CORN
      ordinal ($\rho\,0.513$) vs.\ our distribution head ($\rho\,0.578$);
      the distribution head additionally yields free uncertainty.
\item \emph{Ensemble drop-one} --- removing A barely changes the blend
      ($\rho\,0.589$), removing C collapses it ($\rho\,0.451$): C carries the
      blend, A is the weakest member.
\item \emph{Mean-pool + LLRD + rank loss} --- together raised B from a
      noisy $\rho\approx0.56$ (seed range $0.50$--$0.61$) to a stable
      $\rho\,0.610$ (seed range $0.56$--$0.60$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}
% =====================================================================
\textbf{Dataset \& its impact.} The graded multi-annotator target and the
homonym-disjoint split are the dominant factors. They cap lexical methods near
chance-plus and reward semantic generalisation; they also impose an
irreducible ceiling --- when a third of items have annotator $\sigma\ge1.2$
no system can be uniformly ``right''. This is why accuracy@std rises more
readily than Spearman.

\textbf{Approach trade-offs.} Component~A needs no training and generalises
but its ceiling is the NLI checkpoint's sense sensitivity (weakest member,
$\rho\,0.381$). Component~B is the strongest per-item once mean-pooling, LLRD
and the rank-aligned loss stabilise it ($\rho\,0.610$, \emph{OK}).
Component~C's distribution objective is the most data-efficient use of the
labels and yields free uncertainty, at a slightly noisier scalar read-out
($\rho\,0.578$).

\textbf{Comparison to literature \& SOTA.} There is no public SOTA for this
2026 task yet, so we compare against (i) the official constant baselines,
(ii) our milestone lexical systems, and (iii) the cited methods we
re-implement (gloss-informed encoding, zero-shot-via-NLI, CORN). Our best
configuration improves Spearman from $0.166$ (best milestone baseline) to
$0.610$.

\textbf{Limitations.} (1) The annotator-disagreement ceiling on both metrics.
(2) \emph{The ensemble does not beat its best single component on dev}
($0.588$ vs.\ B's $0.610$): the 276-row homonym-disjoint hold-out is too
small to tune three convex weights without overfitting; the guard prevents a
catastrophic blend but cannot recover the lost $0.02\,\rho$. (3) Continuous
predictions exploit the scorer's tolerance; we report the integer-only number
too for transparency. (4) NLI templates are hand-designed, not searched.

\textbf{If we had more time/resources.} A larger or repeated-CV hold-out (or
nested cross-validation) for less noisy ensemble weights; continued masked-LM
pre-training on SemCor/CoarseWSD; a learned (not grid) blender; prompt search
and few-shot retrieval for Component~A; back-translation augmentation.

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% =====================================================================
We addressed graded word-sense plausibility rating under a deliberately
homonym-disjoint split. Our contribution is a three-way system combining a
training-free NLI prior, a gloss-informed supervised regressor, and a
\textbf{novel Likert-distribution head} that models annotator disagreement
and emits a continuous rating with calibrated uncertainty, trained with a
\textbf{rank-aligned loss} and stabilised with \textbf{layer-wise LR decay}
--- our own methodological additions on top of the cited recipes. The best
configuration reaches accuracy@std~$0.767$ / Spearman~$0.610$ (\emph{OK}), a
$\sim$$3.7\times$ Spearman gain over the milestone baselines, and the project
is fully reproducible from \texttt{final/src/run\_all.py}. The headline
limitation --- a small hold-out capping ensemble gains --- is itself an
informative, honestly reported finding.
